# Reference check — the 33 references added on 26 September

The supervisor's checklist asks you to check every reference yourself rather
than trust ones an AI tool produced. These are the ones added in the checklist
pass; the original eighteen were already checked (Appendix G).

For each: open the link, and confirm that the **title, authors, year and
venue** match the entry, and that the page or DOI resolves. Where the thesis
quotes a figure from the source, the section is noted so you can check that too.
Tick the box as you go (in VS Code, edit `[ ]` to `[x]`).

Figures taken from sources, worth a second look:
- Manduchi and Kurniawan (2011): 307 respondents; 13 % head-level accidents at
  least monthly; 7 % falls at least monthly; 23 % of head-level accidents with
  medical consequences; 36 % of respondents reporting falls with medical
  consequences (§1.1.1, §3.3.4). The PDF is linked.
- Luetzenburg, Kroon and Bjørk (2021): ±1 cm for objects larger than 10 cm
  (§2.5.5, §5.3).
- Bohannon (1997): comfortable walking speed 1.27–1.46 m/s (Table 3.13).
- Sears and Hanson (2012): non-representative users acceptable for preliminary
  evaluations, not for archival claims (§2.5.5).
- Northcutt, Athalye and Mueller (2021): label errors in the test sets of ten
  benchmarks (§5.2.5).
- Dakopoulos and Bourbakis (2010): 22 wearable aids surveyed (§2.3).
