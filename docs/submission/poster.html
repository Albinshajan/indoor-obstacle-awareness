The gapThe cane is excellent, and blind in three specific ways

It detects obstacles only on contact, says nothing about what they are, and covers nothing above sweep height. Nor has the field closed it: the closest comparable system reports distance accuracy as a boolean.

Result 1The accuracy–latency trade-off did not appear

YOLO26n won every axis; all three candidates exceeded 75 fps on an A15. And 43% of pipeline latency is not inference — buffer conversion and resizing cost nearly as much.

Result 3 · unplannedDetection, not depth, was the bottleneck

A usable detection appeared in only 37% of 102 frames; pole has zero training instances and the taxonomy's second-highest hazard weight.

A second corpus found the object in 37 of 102 frames against 12 — but boxed empty floor as door.

The sensing is good enough. The detection is not yet.

Median depth over each box's lower third feeds bearing, closing rate and time-to-contact into a hazard-weighted urgency score, gated by 3-of-5 confirmation. Derived layer: 0.066 ms/frame.

Stated limitations

RQ3 is unanswered. Four taxonomy classes have no training instances. The urgency weighting is unvalidated — no user has confirmed that what it scores as urgent feels so. Dim lighting untested.

What follows

Run the evaluation — approval exists and is unused, and the three feedback conditions are already switchable. Obtain a source carrying pole and retrain. Establish what "fast enough" means for a walking user.
