# Recruitment message — decisions taken and what remains

## Settled, 15 September

| Item | Decision |
|---|---|
| Audio/video recording | **None.** No filming, photography or audio capture at any point |
| Participants | **Five** |
| Age | **18 or over** |
| Location | **On the Middlesex University Dubai campus** |
| Safety | A volunteer stays beside each participant throughout, monitoring for safety |
| Payment | None |

**No recording** resolves the contradiction in the approved form — §2.6 stated
sessions "will be video recorded" while §4.11 was marked **No** — in favour of
§4.11. Collecting less personal data than was approved raises no ethical
difficulty and needs no amendment, on the same reasoning as the title/scope
point in §3.2.1. It also removes a data-protection obligation for data the
study does not need: the measures are timings, outcomes and post-task comments,
all of which are written notes.

## Still open

**`[DURATION]`.** Do one dry run — yourself or a friend, not as a participant —
and put the real figure in before sending. Two conditions plus briefing,
consent, tasks and debrief; thirty minutes is plausible but untimed.
Understating it and then overrunning is the quickest way to lose the fifth
person.

**`[DATES]`.** Not fixed. The message works without them if you ask for
availability instead — replace "on [DATES]" with "next week, around your
timetable" and settle times individually. That lets the message go out before
the dates are known, which matters, because recruitment lead time is the one
part of this that cannot be compressed.

## Note on status

The evaluation is **not committed**. The message can go out to gauge interest
without obliging anyone, including you, to run it — but if it does go out,
anyone who says yes should get a straight answer if it is later called off.
Chapters 1, 2, 4 and 5 currently state that the evaluation was not conducted,
which remains true as of now.

## Two things the message deliberately does

**It describes obstacle awareness, not navigation.** The approved form says
participants will undertake "navigation and obstacle avoidance tasks", but the
delivered system does no route guidance. The message describes what
participants will actually do.

**It states the feasibility-versus-usability limit to the participants
themselves.** Unusual in a recruitment message, and deliberate. Chapter 2
criticises this literature for blurring that line; telling volunteers what
their contribution can and cannot support is the same commitment applied to the
people doing the work.
