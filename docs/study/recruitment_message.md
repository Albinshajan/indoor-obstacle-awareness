## Long version — email

**Subject:** Volunteers needed (5) — 30-minute study, MSc dissertation

Hi [NAME],

I'm looking for five volunteers for a short study that forms part of my MSc
Data Science and AI dissertation at Middlesex University Dubai, supervised by
Dr Ikram Ur Rehman.

### What the project is

I've built a smartphone app that detects obstacles indoors — doors, chairs,
table edges, things on the floor — works out how far away they are, and
communicates that through audio cues and vibration. It's designed as a
supplement to a white cane for blind and visually impaired users, not a
replacement for one.

What I need to find out is whether **adding vibration to audio actually helps**,
or whether audio alone does the job. That comparison is the reason I need
people rather than measurements.

### What you'd actually do

- Read an information sheet and sign a consent form.
- Wear a blindfold and walk a short indoor course, holding the phone at chest
  height, reacting to what it tells you about obstacles ahead.
- Do this twice — once with audio only, once with audio and vibration. The
  order varies between participants.
- Answer a few questions afterwards about how it felt.

Total time: **about [DURATION]**. Location: on the Middlesex University Dubai
campus. I'm running sessions on [DATES] and can work around your timetable.

### Safety, honestly stated

Walking blindfolded carries some risk of bumping into something, tripping, or
simply feeling uneasy. I'm not going to pretend otherwise, so here's how it's
handled:

- The course is a controlled indoor space with clear walkways and low, soft
  obstacles placed deliberately. No stairs, no traffic, no real hazards.
- You get a full safety briefing before anything starts.
- **A sighted volunteer stays beside you for the entire task**, monitoring for
  safety and ready to step in immediately.
- You set the pace. Slow is fine.
- You can stop or withdraw at any moment, without giving a reason — before,
  during, or up to **seven days afterwards**, in which case your data is
  deleted.

The study involves no medication, no physical treatment, and nothing invasive.
It is not a test of you, and there is no way to perform it badly — I'm testing
the app, not your ability.

### Eligibility

- **18 or over**
- Sighted (corrective lenses are fine)
- Able to walk unaided for a few minutes
- No condition that makes walking with restricted vision unsafe for you —
  balance or mobility difficulties, dizziness, or anything similar. If you're
  unsure, tell me and we'll talk it through rather than guess.

### What is recorded

I record how you complete the tasks — timings and outcomes — and your comments
afterwards, as written notes. **Nothing is filmed, photographed or
audio-recorded at any point.** Your name is not attached to the results. Data is held in encrypted, password-protected cloud
storage accessible only to me and my supervisor, and the write-up reports
findings across participants, never individuals.

### One thing I want to be clear about

You'd be helping me establish that the system **works**, technically. Because
you're sighted and blindfolded rather than blind or visually impaired, this
study cannot and will not be reported as evidence that the app is usable by
blind or visually impaired people — their experience, spatial strategies and
cane technique differ substantially from a sighted person wearing a blindfold
for half an hour. I'll say exactly that in the dissertation, and I'm saying it
here so you know what your time is contributing to.

Participation is entirely voluntary, and there's no payment or reimbursement.
Saying no costs you nothing with me.

If you're interested, reply and I'll send the participant information sheet and
consent form in advance so you can read them before deciding.

Thanks very much,
