### Late find, 15 Sept evening — the orientation fix that saves Objective 2

Running the pipeline against **real capture-app output** for the first time (the
16 frames already on the device) exposed a fault that synthetic testing could
not: the detector found **1 box in 16 frames**.

Cause: the capture app stores pixels in ARKit's unrotated sensor frame with a
cosmetic EXIF orientation tag of 6 — a portrait-held phone needs a 90-degree
clockwise rotation to appear upright. The detector was being handed every scene
lying on its side, which is out of distribution for an upright indoor training
corpus.

Measured on the four frames that were actually aimed at an object:

| | as stored | rotated upright |
|---|---|---|
| frames with a detection | **0 of 4** | **4 of 4** |
| chair frame | no detection | **chair, 0.96** |

Fixed in `tools/orientation.py`, imported by both `predict_boxes.py` (detects on
the upright frame, writes boxes in upright pixel space) and `distance_eval.py`
(rotates depth and confidence maps to match). Verified end to end on the real
frames: four detector-sourced rows, LiDAR reading 1.13 m for the chair and
0.45 / 1.54 / 3.10 m for the doors — all plausible for the room they were shot
in.

**Without this, tomorrow's 113 frames would have produced almost no detector
boxes**, everything would have fallen back to the centred-ROI heuristic, and
`CLAUDE.md` is explicit that those numbers must not be cited. Objective 2 would
have had no citable result.

Note also: `roi_source` is field 12 of `results_long.csv`, not 13.

## 4. Corrections made — things previously recorded wrongly

Worth reading before trusting any older note.

**`CLAUDE.md` was wrong about the ethics form.** It said no checkbox carried a
recorded answer and that the supervisor block was blank. Both false: the form is
substantively complete and the supervisor signed it on 11/08/2026. Only the
peer-reviewer and REC decision blocks are blank.

**The pitch HUD was not broken.** An earlier claim in this session — that
`eulerAngles.x` could not be trusted because a portrait-held phone sits at
roll ≈ ±90° — was disproved by twelve real captures: `camera_elevation_deg` and
`eulerAngles.x` agree to two decimal places on every frame. The forward-vector
measure was kept anyway (equivalent, no axis-ordering assumption, now
cross-checked), and the code comments were corrected. **Roll reading −86° to
−93° in portrait is correct and must not be "fixed"** — ARKit defines the camera
frame in landscape-right.

**Chapter 3 wrongly said no ethics amendment was needed.** The approved form
names only COCO and the self-collected set; the detectors were fine-tuned on the
Roboflow corpus. §3.2.2 now describes the actual sources, without the
"amendment required" framing, per the decision above.

## 5. Chapter 2 source verification

Seven figures were carried from the earlier draft unverified. Full record in
`docs/thesis/source_verification.md`.

**Six confirmed**, three with corrections to how they were described:

- Khilnani and Devesh report **mAP 53.4 % *and* AP50 71.7 %**. The draft quoted
  only the higher figure. 53.4 % is the one comparable to Chapter 4's mAP50-95.
- Tokmurziyev's 81.3 % is recognition across **13 haptic patterns**, not
  directional cues.
- Okolo is a **Raspberry Pi smart stick** plus a separate Android GPS app, not a
  smartphone with a sensor attached.
- Zhao and Zhang's 85 % is measured at intersections **30 m wide** — nearly four
  times the 8 m range at which 96.3 % was established — so that drop conflates
  scene complexity with operating beyond validated range. Their **94 % in low
  light at the same 8 m range** is the cleaner, single-variable comparison and
  §2.5.1 now leads on it.

**One removed:** the Crivello **44 %** attributed to usability testing. Public
text from that survey attaches 44 % to solutions *requiring a preliminary
training phase*. Could not verify — Taylor & Francis, CNR IRIS and curl with a
browser agent all returned 403 despite the article being hybrid OA. Domain D now
rests on the survey's own stated headline findings instead. **Optional:** if
institutional access becomes available, search the PDF for `44` and `usability`;
if a real usability percentage exists, it can be restored.

**Two findings from the Okolo paper strengthen the thesis** and are now used in
§2.5.2, §5.3 and §6.3:

- its nine "obstacles" are COCO classes — person, car, bus, truck, chair, TV,
  couch, bottle, refrigerator — **none a door, step, stair or pole**, which
  evidences §3.3.1's dataset argument rather than contradicting it;
- it reports **distance accuracy as a boolean `True`** for all nine objects — no
  magnitude, no units, no ground truth. The strongest support available for
  Objective 2's gap claim.
