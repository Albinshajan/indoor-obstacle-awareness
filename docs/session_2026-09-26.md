# Session record — 21 to 26 September 2026

Follows `session_2026-09-18.md`. One long thread: the detector was measured at
the threshold it actually runs at, that measurement raised a question about the
data, and answering it took seven retraining runs and changed what the thesis
claims about benchmarks.

## What changed, in order

**1. The detector was evaluated at its operating point** (`tools/detection_metrics.py`,
new §3.5.3, §4.1.7, Figures 4.1–4.2 — the first figures in Chapter 4). mAP
averages over every confidence threshold; the prototype runs at one, 0.30. At
that point YOLO26n names **89 %** of the objects it finds correctly and finds
**21 %** of those present. The errors are misses, not confusions, and no
threshold lifts recall past 41 %. Before computing anything new the script
reproduces the Colab AP figures to four decimal places, so this is the same
evaluation Chapter 4 already reported, seen at one point.

**2. The "spurious" boxes were audited** (`tools/audit_spurious.py`), because
precision measured against annotations is a claim about the annotations too.
Of 581 boxes counted spurious, 180 sit on an object of their own class. Of a
sample from those overlapping nothing annotated, about half are real objects
nobody labelled — mostly people and chairs.

**3. So the training labels were completed and the model retrained**
(`tools/relabel_missing.py`). A YOLO26x COCO teacher added 2,204
person/chair/table boxes to the training split, validation untouched. Recall on
`person` doubled (0.155 → 0.341) and on `chair` (0.098 → 0.198), **measured
against the original validation labels**, so the gain is in finding the objects
the annotators did label. The large-person anomaly disappeared. §5.2.2's
annotation suspicion is now a tested result.

**4. The AI Builders source, recorded as unobtainable since the corpus was
frozen, was found** on Kaggle (`thepbordin/indoor-object-detection`, GPL-3.0)
and imported as a separate tree (`tools/import_ai_builders.py`, §3.3.7). It was
**not** merged into `data/processed/merged`: re-merging would redraw the scene
split and invalidate every measurement already taken.

**5. Seven training runs** (`tools/train_ablation.py`), one change at a time,
all local on MPS at about six hours each. Results: `results/objective1_ablation_findings.md`,
written into §4.1.8 and §5.2.5.

**6. The result that changed the thesis's argument.** The combined recipe wins
every photograph benchmark — and draws confident `door` boxes over empty
corridor floor when pointed where the tape-measured captures were taken. The
prototype therefore keeps the model Chapter 4 measures. Both retrained models
are bundled in the app as selectable, labelled experimental, so the comparison
can be repeated on hardware.

## Decisions worth not relitigating

- **The app default stays `yolo26n`.** Every Objective 2 and 3 figure was
  measured with it, and the alternatives regress in the deployment setting.
- **COCO images were not carried into the combined recipe.** Gain +0.020 micro
  F1, interval −0.005 to 0.047, and it doubles training time.
- **Training longer was never tested**, and should not be: validation loss
  rises from epoch 1 in all three original checkpoints.
- **`cabinetDoor` and `refrigeratorDoor` are deliberately unmapped.** Mapping a
  cabinet front to `door` would teach a cabinet as a doorway.
- **Mosaic is retracted as a design decision, not as an argument.** §3.4's
  reasoning about viewpoints still stands; the measurement beats it.

## Things this session got wrong, and how they were caught

- **Three scoring runs failed silently.** `--weights` flags were passed through
  an unquoted zsh variable, so argparse received one long argument; the output
  filter hid the error. Found by running one command alone and looking at its
  exit code. `tools/score_ablation.sh` now exists, in bash, with failures
  reported rather than filtered.
- **AI Builders was described as "NYU-derived home interiors"** in a commit
  message and a findings file. Its own images contradict that — a railway
  platform, a shop, web-style file names. Corrected; the file names are now
  characterised from the data.
- **The poster's Panel 5 said "the 73 frames where the detector found the
  object".** The figure is 38 of 102. Fixed.
- **A poster line said a second corpus "lifted coverage to 37 %"**, which reads
  as the same measure as Panel 5's "37 % of 102 frames" and is not: one counts
  any usable box, the other counts frames where the photographed object is
  found. Reworded before it was printed.
- **Adding two paragraphs pushed the A5 poster onto a second page.** Caught by
  printing it to PDF and counting page objects, not by looking at it.
