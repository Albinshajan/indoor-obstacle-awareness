**Inconsistencies within the application documents.** All are addressed in
§3.2.3 and summarised here, so that a reader checking the appendix against the
methodology finds them resolved rather than unexplained.

- **Recording.** Form §2.6 states that sessions "will be video recorded"; form
  §4.11 ("Will you audio or video record interviews and/or observations?") is
  marked **No**; form §4.10 (consent from people appearing in visual data) is
  marked **Yes**. The debriefing sheet (A.4), dated before the information and
  consent sheets, also refers to a video recording. The information and consent
  sheets (A.2, A.3) state that no recording is made. The protocol adopted
  follows form §4.11 and those sheets: **no audio, video or photographic record
  of participants is made**, and measures are written notes.
- **Participant count.** Form §2.6 specifies five participants and form §3.1
  specifies 10–15. The governing figure is **five**, as the information sheet
  also states.
- **Post-task instrument.** Form §2.6 describes post-task interviews; the
  information and consent sheets describe a short post-task questionnaire.
- **Title.** The form and the debriefing sheet carry the earlier title, "A
  Smartphone-Based Navigation Assistant"; the information and consent sheets
  carry the thesis title.

The three sheets are reproduced as submitted, without correction: the drafting

### A.2 Participant information sheet

**PARTICIPANT INFORMATION SHEET**

**Middlesex University Dubai**

*Note on risk classification: Middlesex\'s standard 'Minimal Risk' PIS template explicitly excludes studies involving experiments or observation studies, even where no recording is made. This project asks participants to complete a blindfolded obstacle-course task while being observed and timed by the researcher, which likely still falls outside a strict 'Minimal Risk' classification, even without video/audio recording. This document uses the Minimal Risk template\'s structure as the closest available fit -- please confirm the correct risk tier and any additional required template with your supervisor before submission.*

**1. Project/Study Title**

A Smartphone-Based Prototype for Indoor Obstacle Awareness: Real-Time Object Detection, Distance Estimation, and Audio-Haptic Feedback for Blind and Visually Impaired Users

**2. Invitation Paragraph**

You are being invited to take part in a research study. Before you decide, it is important for you to understand why the research is being done and what it will involve. Please take time to read the following information carefully, and discuss it with others if you wish. Ask the researcher if there is anything that is not clear, or if you would like more information. Take time to decide whether or not you wish to take part. Thank you for reading this.

**3. What is the Purpose of the Project/Study?**

This project is building a smartphone app that helps blind and visually impaired people notice obstacles indoors, using the phone\'s camera to detect nearby objects and warning the user through sound and vibration. Because this is an early prototype, we are testing it with sighted volunteers wearing a blindfold, rather than approaching blind or visually impaired people directly at this stage. This helps us check whether the app\'s warnings are clear and given with enough time before we take it further.

**4. Why Have I Been Chosen?**

You have been invited because you are a fellow student or personal contact of the researcher, aged 18 or over, and available to take part. We are aiming for around 5 participants in total. There are no special requirements beyond being 18 or over, comfortable wearing a blindfold and walking short distances with a sighted assistant beside you, and having no medical condition (e.g. balance or inner-ear conditions) that would make this unsafe for you.

**5. Do I Have to Take Part?**

No. Taking part is entirely voluntary. You may withdraw at any point before, during, or within 7 days after your session, without giving a reason and without any negative consequences. If you withdraw within this window, any data already collected from you will be deleted.

**6. What Will I Have to Do?**

If you agree to take part, you will wear a blindfold and use the prototype app to navigate a short, pre-arranged obstacle course in a controlled indoor space. A sighted assistant will be with you at all times for your safety. There will be no audio or video recording. Instead, the researcher will note basic details of your session by hand -- such as how long the task took and whether any obstacles were missed -- linked only to a participant code, not your name. Afterwards, you will be asked to complete a short questionnaire about your experience. The whole session is expected to take approximately 20--30 minutes.

**7. What Are the Possible Risks or Benefits of Taking Part?**

The main risk is a minor chance of trips or light collisions while blindfolded. This is minimised by using only soft, padded obstacles, clearing the space of anything hazardous, and having a sighted assistant supervise you throughout; you may remove the blindfold and stop at any point if you feel uncomfortable. There is no guaranteed direct benefit to you from taking part. However, your feedback may help improve how this kind of assistive app communicates with its users, which may ultimately benefit blind and visually impaired people.

**8. Data Protection and Confidentiality**

This study does not involve any audio or video recording. The data collected consists of: (a) brief handwritten notes made by the researcher during your session (e.g. task completion time, whether any obstacle was missed), and (b) your answers to the post-task questionnaire. Both will be linked only to a participant code (e.g. 'P1'), not your name. The key linking your name to your code will be stored separately from this data and deleted once the dissertation has been marked. Your data will not be shared or published in a form that could identify you.

**9. What Will Happen to the Results of the Research Study?**

The results of this study will be used as part of the researcher\'s MSc dissertation at Middlesex University Dubai. Findings may also be presented at conferences or in academic publications, always in anonymised, aggregated form with no individual results identifiable.

## Appendix E — Objective 2 distance study

The full distance-evaluation output (`results/objective2/`): per-frame
long-form results with the region-of-interest source recorded on every row
(`results_long.csv`), the summary by method, class, distance bin and lighting
(`results_summary.csv`), and the detector coverage report (`boxes.md`).

The capture-set audit trail is reproduced with it
(`data/raw/own_capture/_correction_log.txt`): 121 frames pulled from the
device, 19 excluded as smoke-test or practice shots and preserved rather than
deleted, and 12 relabelled to their true distance where the distance field had
not been advanced before the next distance was shot. Every exclusion and
relabelling is individually listed, because a clean set with no record of what
was removed is not auditable.

`results_long.csv` contains rows from both the detector-driven regions of
interest and the centred fallback. Only the `roi_source = detector` subset is
reported in §4.2. The fallback rows are retained because deleting them would
remove the evidence of how many frames the detector missed, which is §4.2.1's
finding.

## Appendix I — Code and data submission

The code is submitted as the project repository. It contains every tool used to
prepare the data, train and export the models, evaluate them and build this
report (`tools/`); the iOS application, including the prototype, the capture
application and the benchmark harness (`ios/ObstacleAwareness/`); every result
file cited in Chapter 4 (`results/`); and the sources of this report
(`docs/thesis/`). Dataset images are not redistributed: the Roboflow and
AI Builders sets and COCO are available from their publishers under the
licences in Table 3.2, and §3.3 describes how each was obtained and
processed. The tape-measured capture set is researcher-owned and contains no
personal data other than the researcher's own feet in some frames.

The main results can be regenerated with:

```
python tools/prepare_data.py merge ...          # corpus and scene split (§3.3)
python tools/detection_metrics.py               # operating-point evaluation (§4.1.7)
python tools/predict_boxes.py ...               # detector boxes on the captures (§4.2.1)
python tools/distance_eval.py ... --run-monocular   # distance errors (§4.2)
python tools/distance_uncertainty.py            # intervals and placement (§4.2)
bash tools/verify_decision_layer.sh             # 45 decision-layer checks (§4.3.2)
python tools/check_citations.py                 # citation and cross-reference check
bash tools/build_thesis.sh pdf                  # this report
```

Each script's own header documents its arguments.
